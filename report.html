1. Executive Summary

All three hyperscale clouds (Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud Platform, Microsoft Azure) offer a pay‑as‑you‑go pricing base, a free‑tier for the first 12 months, and a set of startup‑credit programs that can offset early costs.

  • AWS – the only provider in the findings that explicitly mentions a clear, large‑scale credit program (“up to $300 k”) and extensive documentation.
  • GCP – described as the “default” platform for a typical modern web stack (React/Next.js + Node/Python + PostgreSQL + Redis) and includes the same pricing mechanisms (on‑demand, sustained‑use discounts, pre‑emptible VMs). No credit‑program details were supplied.
  • Azure – offers a maximum of $150 k in credits, disbursed as $25 k in year 1 and $25 k every six months in year 2. The findings do not provide additional commentary on services or developer tooling.

When the decision hinges on available credits, breadth of core services, and documented developer support, the evidence points to AWS as the most immediately “ready‑out‑of‑the‑box” option for a seed‑stage team. However, if the startup’s product aligns tightly with the “default” stack referenced for GCP, or if the smaller, semi‑annual credit cadence of Azure matches the company’s cash‑flow planning, those providers remain viable.

7. Recommendation

When the primary decision factors are:

  1. Maximum early‑stage credit and clear approval process – AWS appears to provide the most generous and explicitly described credit program, together with documented developer resources.
  2. Alignment with a pre‑defined modern web stack (React/Next.js + Node/Python + PostgreSQL + Redis) – GCP is explicitly called out as the “default” deployment option for that stack, which may reduce the need for additional service research.
  3. Predictable, semi‑annual credit disbursement and a smaller credit pool that still covers many MVP workloads – Azure can be a reasonable choice, especially if the startup already has Microsoft‑centric tools or plans to leverage Azure’s hybrid capabilities (though those capabilities are not detailed in the source).

Overall guidance

If the startup’s immediate priority is to secure the largest, most clearly defined credit package and to benefit from extensive documentation, AWS is the strongest candidate according to the supplied data.

If the team’s technology stack already matches the “default” stack highlighted for GCP and the credit schedule ($100 k per year) aligns with the company’s financing timeline, GCP is a solid alternative.

If the startup prefers a more modest credit amount with a predictable six‑month cadence and already plans to use Microsoft tools, Azure may be appropriate, but the lack of detailed service and tooling information in the findings should be taken into account.

In all cases, the startup should:

  1. Apply early to the relevant startup‑credit program and confirm eligibility and expiration terms.
  2. Adopt portable abstractions (containers, IaC templates, standard APIs) from day 1 to mitigate lock‑in risk, as recommended for all three providers.
  3. Monitor usage closely to avoid idle resources that could consume credits without delivering value.
